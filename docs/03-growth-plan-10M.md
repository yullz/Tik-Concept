# 03 · Growth Plan — 10,000,000 views in month one

## The honest math first

Short-form views follow a power law: a handful of outliers carry the month. A plan that
needs the *median* video to do the work fails. The plan below does two things: it raises
the floor with volume across platforms and languages, and it maximises the number of
high-quality lottery tickets (distinct hooks) so three or more videos can break a million.

### Scenario table (English main accounts plus two dubbed accounts)

| Surface | Posts / month | Median views (base) | Floor views | Expected breakouts ≥ 1M (base → stretch) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok EN | 60 (2/day) | 20,000 | 1.2M | 1 → 3 |
| YouTube Shorts EN | 60 (2/day) | 15,000 | 0.9M | 1 → 2 |
| Instagram + Facebook Reels EN | 30 (1/day) | 8,000 | 0.25M | 0 → 1 |
| TikTok ES (dubbed) | 30 (1/day) | 15,000 | 0.45M | 0 → 1 |
| TikTok PT-BR (dubbed) | 30 (1/day) | 12,000 | 0.35M | 0 → 1 |
| **Totals** | **210 posts** | | **≈ 3.2M floor** | **2 → 8 breakouts** |

- Base case: floor 3.2M + two breakouts at ~1.5M = **~6M**.
- Stretch case (what the plan is built for): floor 3.2M + six breakouts averaging 1.3M = **~11M**.
- The difference between base and stretch is entirely hook quality and the number of distinct hooks tested. That is why the production pipeline makes hooks cheap to vary.

Ten million is reachable in month one only with (a) ≥ 150 posts across surfaces, (b) at least two language accounts, and (c) ≥ 4 breakouts. We should state that plainly to ourselves and aim for the stretch case.

### Why 210 posts does not mean 210 productions

- **30 master episodes** are produced (one per day).
- Each master yields: TikTok EN, Shorts EN, Reels EN (same file, different metadata and cover).
- **No re-uploads, no hook swaps on the same body.** TikTok's originality rules (and the
  Creator Rewards requirement for original content) penalise duplicated or recycled posts. Every
  post is a distinct cut: the second TikTok post of a day is a different episode, never a variant.
- Each master is **re-cut** for ES and PT-BR: the narration is re-recorded in the locked voice in
  that language, the pipeline re-times every cut to the new narration, regenerates the cards,
  cover and file suffix. Visually it shares keyframes; structurally it is a different edit on a
  different account in a different language.
- Weekly: 4 **reply episodes** built from the best comment objections (61–65 s, one phase, new
  script, new shots). These are the cheapest videos we make and historically the most-followed.
- Hook testing happens **across episodes**, not within one: each week's ten episodes use at
  least four different hook patterns from `docs/02-format-beat-sheet.md`; the Monday loop
  reads which patterns held the 5-second mark.

## Platform mechanics we exploit

**TikTok**
- Post at two fixed slots (test 07:00 and 18:00 in the target time zone in week 1, then lock the best two).
- First 60 minutes: reply to every comment that contains an argument; pin the dilemma comment (which takes a side).
- Use TikTok's *reply with video* on the best objection within 24 h (this is the reply-episode source).
- Titles: the hook sentence + "(Part N)" when in a series; 3–5 hashtags max: one niche (`#alternatehistory`), one broad (`#history`), one format (`#whatif`), one series tag (`#neverfell`).
- Flag AI-generated content. It does not suppress reach; getting flagged by others does.
- Keep the first frame text-light; the cover is chosen in the pipeline (`cover_time`).

**YouTube Shorts**
- Title ≤ 60 characters, the hook sentence verbatim. Description: the dilemma, "Alternate timeline" label, sources line, series playlist link.
- Use the Shorts "related video" link to point at the previous episode in the series.
- Upload 2/day at 12-hour spacing. Shorts rewards consistency more than TikTok does.
- Enable altered-content disclosure.

**Instagram / Facebook Reels**
- Same master, cover with title text, post once a day, cross-post to Facebook (Facebook Reels still has under-served long-form watch-time distribution for history content).

**Languages**
- Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese first (largest TikTok history audiences outside English, and dubbing quality is strong). Hindi and Indonesian in month two if ES/PT hold a 15k median.
- Each language is its own account with the same name and sigil; descriptions and cards translated in the episode YAML (`localizations`).

## Comment engineering (ethically: real dilemmas, not rage bait)

- The ending is a dilemma with two defensible sides (`docs/02-format-beat-sheet.md`).
- The pinned comment takes one side with one fact.
- The second comment (posted from a team account) takes the other side with one fact.
- Reply to the first ten real arguments with one sentence each. Never argue in paragraphs.
- Collect the best objection per episode into `renders/<episode>/replies.md` for the reply episode.
- Never fake inaccuracies to farm corrections. A deliberate error is slop with extra steps, and the audience notices.

## The weekly iteration loop

Every Monday, pull the per-post metrics into `analytics/week-NN.csv` (template in `docs/04-production-pipeline.md`) and answer four questions:
1. Which hook pattern had the best 3-second hold? Write two more hooks in that pattern this week.
2. Which pillar had the best completion? Add one episode to it.
3. Which episode had the highest comment rate? Its objection becomes a reply episode.
4. Which episode scored lowest on the taste scorecard but did well, or vice versa? Adjust the scorecard, not the taste.
